# Reporting phishing and suspicious emails

Phishing emails try to trick you into giving a password, clicking a link or paying a fake invoice. Report anything that looks wrong; you will never be in trouble for a false alarm.

## Warning signs

- Unexpected request to log in, reset a password or confirm account details
- Urgent or threatening language ("your account will be closed today")
- Sender address that does not match the company, or only one letter different
- Unexpected attachment or a link where the hover address differs from the text
- A request to buy gift cards or change bank details, even from a name you know

## What to do

1. **Do not click links or open attachments.**
2. Click **Report Phish** on the Outlook ribbon (or the three dots on mobile). This sends the email to IT for analysis and removes it from your inbox.
3. If the button is missing, forward the email as an attachment to `phishing@acme.example`.
4. Delete the message afterwards.

IT usually replies within 2 hours on working days. If the email was a real attack, it is removed from every mailbox.

## If you already clicked or entered details

Act at once. This is a security incident:

1. Disconnect from Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable.
2. Call the security hotline on **ext. 4999** (24 hours).
3. Do not try to clean it up yourself.

See the Suspected breach, malware or ransomware article.

## Calls and texts

Phishing also arrives as texts and phone calls. Staff are never asked for passwords or MFA codes by IT. Report these to the helpdesk.
