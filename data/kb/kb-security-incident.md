# Suspected breach, malware or ransomware

A security incident is urgent. **Do not try to fix it yourself and do not ask the helpdesk chatbot to.** Contact a person.

## Signs of an incident

- Files renamed or a message demanding payment (ransomware)
- Antivirus alerts you cannot clear, or unknown programs running
- Account activity you did not do: sign-in alerts, password changed, emails sent you did not write
- You clicked a phishing link or entered your password on a strange page
- Data sent to the wrong person, or a colleague asking for access they should not have

## What to do now

1. **Call the security hotline: ext. 4999**, 24 hours a day (external: 020 7946 0999). Say you have a suspected security incident.
2. **Disconnect the device** from Wi-Fi and unplug the network cable. Leave it switched on.
3. **Do not delete anything**, do not restart, and do not plug in USB drives.
4. Write down what you saw and when.
5. Do not discuss it on Teams or email from the affected device.

## What happens next

- The security team takes over and may ask you to hand in the laptop.
- Accounts may be locked and passwords reset for safety. This is expected.
- High-severity incidents are classed as **P1** (see the SLA article) and get a response within 15 minutes.

## Why speed matters

The earlier we know, the less damage is done. If personal data is involved, Acme may have to tell the regulator within 72 hours. See the Data protection article.
